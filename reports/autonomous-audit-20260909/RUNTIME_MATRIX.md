## G36 — child-close ownership and redispatch

The historical [race probe](evidence/cancel-redispatch-race-probe.log) demonstrated cancellation returning in 0.27 ms while the old child remained alive, overlapping redispatch, and old cleanup removing the new lease. That is pre-fix evidence, not the current cancellation contract.

All seven CLI sessions now expose a `closed` lifecycle promise independent of their result. ExecutionEngine retains its active session, concurrency permit and running database state during cancellation until actual close; redispatch is rejected during that interval. Completion and failure handlers also await close, so an early timeout/error result cannot prematurely release ownership. Registry cleanup captures its exact registration, and engine handlers verify session ownership before changing state.

`loop-runtime-stop.test.ts` executes four real-subprocess scenarios: whole-loop stop, worker-pool stop, cancel/redispatch with a deliberately delayed old result, and an early timeout result while a TERM-resistant child is still alive. The redispatch test proves the second child remains registered and stoppable after the first result finally settles. `runtime-semaphore.test.ts` additionally proves stale registry cleanup is harmless and an ENOENT spawn closes without leaking its permit or lease.

## Remaining integration limits

Worker-pool and whole-loop stop now reach engine-owned close-aware cancellation through the existing RuntimeLeaseRegistry. The low-level RuntimeCommandService signal-request method is still a best-effort request; its registered stop callback awaits actual close. Neither a signal request nor a cancelled lease row alone proves termination.

These tests establish closure of the directly owned Node child and its streams, not termination of detached grandchildren, provider-side sessions, containers or remote work. If the OS never emits close, cancellation may remain pending and capacity stays occupied; no bounded completion or automatic recovery is claimed for an uninterruptible child. Real server-crash/provider-session recovery remains unproven beyond the separately documented bounded restart reconciliation.

The previous capability-wide cost/competence heuristics and unsupported Codex fallback were corrected in G58. LoopService.planLoopRun now has a production continuation caller, but its output remains advice, not execution authority; unmatched or unattributable history yields manual selection. ExecutionEngine still takes an explicit executor (default OpenCode). No model-quality or economic advantage is established. Hermes and Deep Agent are not interchangeable substitutes for loop-certified runtimes. Production authenticated browser discovery remains unavailable; no new production/provider status is inferred.
